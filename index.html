One supply chain.

02 / DATA MANAGEMENT

Turn raw records into useful information.

Data is a recorded fact. Information is data placed in a context that helps someone make a decision.

A SIMPLE EXAMPLE
2,000 kgrecorded harvest
2,500 kgconfirmed order

Potential shortfall: 500 kgFlagged before transport is finalised.
01

Collect consistently

Use a shared form with required fields such as farm ID, batch ID, harvest date and weight. Consistent fields make records easier to compare and reconcile.

02

Store responsibly

Keep records organised and access-controlled. Define who can create, view, correct and approve each type of record.

03

Check quality

Information should be accurate, complete, consistent, timely, valid and unique. A polished chart cannot compensate for unreliable source records.

04

Share what each role needs

Processors need inbound quantities and arrival details. Retailers need confirmed available stock and order dates, not every internal farm note.

04 / ETHICS & PRIVACY

Trust is part of the infrastructure.

Farm records can include personal details, payment information, yields and field locations. Information should be handled fairly, transparently and securely.

§
Protection of Personal Information Act

POPIA is South Africa's framework for protecting personal information and regulating how it is processed.

Read about POPIA ↗
01

Be transparent

Explain what is collected, why it is needed, who can access it and how long it will be kept.

02

Limit access by role

Retailers may need confirmed stock, but should not see individual farmers' payment information.

03

Secure the records

Use strong passwords, multi-factor authentication where available, encrypted backups and a breach-response plan.

04

Agree on data sharing

A written agreement should specify permitted uses, access, retention, benefit sharing and what happens when a partnership ends.

05

Keep farmers involved

Farmers should understand how their data is used and have a clear way to ask questions and correct inaccurate personal information.

Advice for review · Not an automated instruction

Start with the basics

  • Digitise essential records first.
  • Pilot one tool for one real problem.
  • Choose mobile-friendly or SMS alerts where appropriate.
  • Check that recommendations fit local conditions.
  • Keep the farmer or manager responsible for the final decision.

AI tools can be expensive, depend on connectivity and perform poorly when data or local calibration is inadequate. The project recommends improving data discipline before adopting complex AI.

06 / ENTERPRISE SYSTEMS

Connect the records, not just the devices.

Integration allows an update in one part of the supply chain to inform the next participant, reducing repeated data entry and delays.

OPTION A

Start with existing tools

Standardise current spreadsheets and forms before replacing everything. Connect the most painful process first.

OPTION B

Configure a platform

Consider a configurable or open-source ERP option when the cooperative has the skills and resources to support it.

OPTION C

Adopt a specialist system

Consider a supply-chain platform such as eProd when traceability, compliance or scale justifies the cost.

Platform costs and features can change. The project report discusses eProd, AGRIFRIK and AgriOS; any real purchase should be based on current vendor information and the cooperative's requirements.

07 / CUSTOMISATION & NEXT STEPS

Make the system fit the people who use it.

A large, expensive rollout is not the recommended starting point. The cooperative should define its biggest information problem, test a small solution and expand only when it proves useful.

Explore the references ↓
01

Understand the need

Consult farmers, cooperative staff, processors and retailers. Prioritise the problems with the greatest impact.

02

Design for local conditions

Plan for intermittent connectivity, different digital skill levels, familiar language and mobile access.

03

Pilot with a small group

Start with a few farms and one processor. Agree on common fields and batch IDs, then train the people entering records.

04

Evaluate and improve

Track a few verifiable indicators, gather feedback, fix problems and expand only if the pilot works.

✳

The central recommendation

Build trustworthy records and clear responsibilities first. Add dashboards, integration and AI only when they solve a defined problem.
